# Problem Statement

## Background

Modern security operations receive alerts from SIEM platforms, network sensors, endpoint agents, threat-intelligence feeds, and human intelligence reports. These sources use different formats, vocabularies, and confidence models, making it difficult to understand whether separate observations are part of the same attack.

## The Problem

Analysts must manually correlate thousands of noisy alerts, separate genuine threats from false positives, and communicate prioritised conclusions to commanders. This manual process is slow, error-prone, and does not scale during a multi-stage intrusion.

## Who is Affected

Defence and enterprise security analysts who triage multi-source alerts, and commanders or incident-response leads who need concise, evidence-backed assessments to prioritise investigation and containment.

## Why It Matters

Delayed correlation allows attackers to progress from initial access to credential theft, remote access, and lateral movement before responders understand the full scope. Analysts also spend excessive time on low-value alerts instead of genuine threats.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Traditional SIEM dashboards show volume, not meaning. They rarely correlate heterogeneous sources into a single investigation narrative, do not consistently explain why alerts were grouped, and often leave commanders without a concise BLUF-style summary or prioritised recommended actions.
